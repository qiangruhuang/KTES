Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Probability-Preserving Active Test Allocation Balances Difficult-Case Discovery and Population Inference under Small Budgets,”** for publication as a **Regular Research** article in the *Journal of Quality Technology*.

Small physical-test campaigns face a recurring quality and reliability design problem: probability sampling supports inference over an operational population, whereas targeted testing is more likely to expose rare or difficult conditions. The manuscript develops c-pKTES-Hedge, a 40-test allocation that embeds three outcome-blind certainty sentinels within an unequal-probability design whose remaining 37 tests retain known positive first-order inclusion probabilities.

The main evidence is an independent confirmation over 1000 finite populations. Relative to a same-budget 15-deterministic-plus-audit comparator, the proposed allocation reduces profile, failure, and critical-tail mean absolute errors and increases definitive-correct decisions, while the paired edge-discovery interval remains compatible with no difference. Three real-data applications then test whether the sampling-and-inference contract can be transported without outcome-driven redesign, including an explicit source-admissibility stop and an endpoint-semantic limitation rather than post hoc repair.

We believe the manuscript fits JQT because its contribution is an applied statistical test-allocation architecture for quality and reliability under severe resource constraints. It combines established unequal-probability, balanced-sampling, and model-assisted ideas in a form intended for practical industry, government, and operational evaluation settings, and it emphasizes reproducible design identity and real-data applicability.

The manuscript is prepared for double-anonymized review. The public benchmark data are cited in the manuscript, and reproduction code and derived artifacts can be supplied to reviewers through blinded supplementary material. Generative-AI use in manuscript preparation is explicitly disclosed in accordance with Taylor & Francis policy.
