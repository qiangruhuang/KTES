# KTES v8 Submission Presentation Gate Audit

**Audit scope:** figures, tables, story compression and claim visibility  
**Method status:** frozen; no new analysis performed  
**Decision:** **PASS WITH REQUIRED PRESENTATION RESTRUCTURING**

## 1. Gate criteria

A submission-ready presentation layer must satisfy five conditions:

1. the final method and the evidence shown in the paper are the same method;
2. the principal controlled result is visually distinguishable from development evidence;
3. external validation does not collapse source admissibility, numerical performance and endpoint validity into one label;
4. adverse or less favorable evidence remains visible;
5. reproducibility detail is retained without overwhelming the main storyline.

## 2. Findings

### G1. Method identity — PASS

The v8 manuscript is centered on frozen `c-pKTES-Hedge`, not the earlier v7 `M=50 / n=10 / eta=0.6` method. The main controlled and external evidence therefore refer to the same final design family.

### G2. Controlled confirmation visibility — PASS, but current presentation is table-heavy

The R5 result supports the intended story: edge hit is statistically compatible with Split15 at the reported precision while profile, failure and critical-tail MAE improve and definitive-correct decisions increase. This should become the dominant quantitative figure rather than remain only Table I.

### G3. External evidence classification — PASS, but needs one integrated figure

The three external studies answer different questions:

- Anti-UAV410: method comparison executed, numerical guardrails pass, but execution clarification remains;
- IDF-DS: source structure fails before outcome access;
- AMOVFLY: numerical gate passes but endpoint semantics limit the engineering claim.

The current manuscript states these correctly, but the reviewer must reconstruct the evidence hierarchy across several subsections. A single layered external-validation figure is required.

### G4. Adverse evidence visibility — PASS

The manuscript retains all important adverse evidence:

- Anti-UAV profile error is worse than SRS by +0.00404;
- the edge-hit CI crosses zero;
- hidden-bias does not demonstrate edge superiority;
- IDF-DS produces no performance result;
- AMOVFLY `F10` is degenerate;
- the later realized design cannot be regenerated byte-for-byte from high-level runtime metadata alone.

No presentation change may suppress these facts.

### G5. Reproducibility hierarchy — PASS, but should be moved mainly to Supplement

The current manuscript contains extensive hashes, provenance rules and execution details. They are scientifically important, but too much of this material in the main paper dilutes the central contribution. The solution is not deletion: retain a compact reproducibility rule in the main text and move the full evidence manifest to Supplementary Information.

## 3. Required restructuring before journal formatting

The paper should be transformed from **four major result tables + long audit prose** to:

- 4 main figures;
- 3 main tables;
- one integrated external-evidence section;
- a structured supplement containing design identities, execution manifests and sensitivity details.

No result needs to be rerun for this restructuring.

## 4. Reviewer-risk audit after restructuring

### Risk A — “The method is a collection of known sampling tools.”

Response: make the contribution explicitly architectural: outcome-blind certainty sentinels + positive-π hedge + probability-preserving inference + fail-closed validation governance under a severe T&E budget. Do not claim novelty for Local Cube or Hájek estimation themselves.

### Risk B — “External validation is inconsistent.”

Response: this is a feature of the evidence architecture, not something to smooth over. Fig. 3 should show that three datasets legitimately yield three different evidence classes.

### Risk C — “AMOVFLY is invalid, so the whole external claim collapses.”

Response: restrict the claim. AMOVFLY supports robustness of the sampling-method comparison under the executed design but not unrestricted engineering endpoint validation. Anti-UAV independently provides a task-effectiveness external comparison, and IDF-DS demonstrates the source gate.

### Risk D — “Why trust a design that cannot be regenerated exactly?”

Response: the project does not accept approximate regeneration. The realized first-order inclusion probabilities and design identities are frozen artifacts. The reproduction failure motivates a stronger provenance contract rather than being hidden.

## 5. Final decision

**PASS WITH REQUIRED PRESENTATION RESTRUCTURING.**

The research evidence is sufficient to proceed to journal-format figures and manuscript compression. No additional experiment is required to pass this presentation gate. A new external dataset would be justified only as a genuinely independent future validation, not as a repair intended to erase the existing Anti-UAV or AMOVFLY limitations.
